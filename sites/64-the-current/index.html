Opinion

Air-conditioning is not
a climate plan

By Marguerite Osei · architect and columnist

Walk through any old town built for hot summers and you will see the answer before anyone has to explain it. Narrow streets that keep each other in shade. Walls thick enough to hold the night's cool until the afternoon. Courtyards with a tree, a little water and a reason to sit still.

We stopped building like that because, for a few decades, we didn't need to. Electricity was cheap, glass was fashionable and the summers were mild enough to get away with it. A machine on the wall could fix whatever the building got wrong.

A cool room that heats the street outside is not a solution. It is a transfer.

That bargain is running out. Air-conditioners pour heat into the street, draw power at exactly the hour the grid is most stretched, and do nothing for the bus stop, the playground or the people who can't afford to run one.

None of this means switching them off. It means treating them as the last line of defence rather than the first: plant the trees, paint the roofs, open the windows at night, and build the next school as if the summers in the chart above are the summers it will have to survive.
