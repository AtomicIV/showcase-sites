The hill

Chalk underfoot,
salt in the air

Monte Rosso is a lump of white limestone with a thin red skin, facing south over the sea. Vine roots go down eight metres to find water, which is why the wines taste of stone before they taste of fruit.

Rita Vela planted the first rows in 1931 with cuttings from her father's garden. Her great-grandchildren run the estate now, and the oldest vines on the east rows are hers. We farm organically, harvest by hand and never make more wine than the hill gives us in a year.

March · Budbreak

The first leaves

The east rows wake first. We walk every vine and count shoots by hand; the ones we leave on decide what the vintage can be.

July · Green harvest

Less, so it's better

We drop a third of the bunches so the rest ripen properly. It feels wasteful every single year, and it's always right.

September · La vendemmia

Picking at dawn

Harvest starts at first light while the grapes are cool. Forty cousins, neighbours and friends, and lunch at one under the pergola.

January · Pruning

The quiet months

The hill goes grey and still. Nonna Rita's granddaughter prunes the oldest rows herself: two canes a vine, no more.
