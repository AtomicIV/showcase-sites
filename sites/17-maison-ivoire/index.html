We make very few things, slowly. A ring from this house has been drawn, cast, set and polished by the same four people, and it will outlast every one of us.

Hélène Varaine, fourth-generation creative director

One ring, 140 hours, four pairs of hands.

I / IV

I.The sketch

Every piece begins in graphite at one oak desk on the third floor of Rue des Orfèvres. We draw at four times scale until the proportions stop arguing with each other.

31drawings for the Aube solitaire

II.The cast

Recycled 18-karat gold is poured at 1,064°C into a lost-wax mould, then filed and shaped by hand. Nothing leaves the bench with a machine edge.

11 hof filing before a stone is chosen

III.The setting

One setter, one stone. Four claws are raised from the metal itself and folded over the girdle under a ten-times loupe, so the diamond sits higher and drinks more light.

4claws, raised from a single band

IV.The polish

Seven grades of polish, the last with walnut-shell powder, until the metal returns the light the stone gives it. Then the piece is signed and numbered inside the band.

7passes, the last one by candlelight
