            § 2  From room to record

            From room to record

            Step 1 of 4

            It asks first.

            Quill starts only after the patient agrees out loud, and the consent is stamped on the note. Say "pause Quill" any time and it stops listening.

            Step 2 of 4

            It sorts as you talk.

            Small talk falls away. Symptoms, findings and decisions land in the right section of your template — SOAP, or whatever your clinic uses.

            Step 3 of 4

            It shows its sources.

            Every line carries a citation back to the transcript, so checking a draft takes seconds instead of rewatching the visit in your head.

            Step 4 of 4

            You sign. It forgets.

            Nothing reaches the record until you sign. Once you do, the audio is deleted and only the note you approved remains.

            § 3  Safeguards

            Built for the exam room,
            not the demo room.

            3.1

            Consent, out loud

            A spoken prompt at the start of every visit. No consent, no recording — and the note says which it was.

            3.2

            A clinician signs

            Drafts are suggestions. Nothing is filed, sent or billed until a named clinician reviews and signs.

            3.3

            Audio doesn't linger

            Recordings are deleted once the note is signed. Only the approved text stays in the record.

            3.4

            Cited, not invented

            Every note line points at the words it came from. If nobody said it, it doesn't go in the note.

            3.5

            Your template

            SOAP, APSO or your own clinic headings. Quill fills your structure instead of imposing its own.

            3.6

            Plays well with records

            Exports to your record system through standard health-data formats, with a copy-paste fallback.
