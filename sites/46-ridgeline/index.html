The kit · Autumn line

Three things
you'll trust

We make eleven products and change them slowly. Each one is weighed on the same brass scale, worn for a season by our guides, and sent back to the bench until nothing on it is decoration.

01 · Pack 1,050g

Talus 38

Roll-top alpine pack · 38 litres

A single aluminium stay, a frame sheet you can sleep on, and a roll-top that compresses down to a day pack after you've eaten the food. The hip belt is cut from one piece, so there's no seam to fail on a long descent.

  • Volume32–38 L
  • Carryup to 14 kg
  • Fabric210D recycled
  • Price€245
02 · Shell 310g

Scree Shell

Three-layer storm jacket

Cut long in the back and short at the hip so it clears your pack belt. A helmet-sized hood with a wired peak, pit zips you can open with gloves on, and a membrane free of PFAS from the first prototype.

  • Hydrostatic head28,000 mm
  • Breathability30k g/m²
  • Packs toa 1 L bottle
  • Price€310
03 · Shelter 1,380g

Cirque 2

Two-person, four-season tent

One pole hub, two doors, and a fly that pitches first so the inner stays dry when you arrive in the rain. Guy-outs sit low on the panels, where wind loads actually land above the treeline.

  • Floor2.9 m²
  • Pitch timeunder 4 min
  • Wind rated90 km/h
  • Price€540

The Larch Traverse · 62 km · 4 days

Where we test

1,184 mkm 0.0 of 62

Day 1 · Workshop to Tarn Hut

A long, easy walk in

We leave from the workshop door. Forest road, then larch meadows. Everything new gets carried on day one, when legs are fresh and complaints are honest.

18 km+1,240 m

Day 2 · Over Grauspitz Col

The shell test

The col collects weather from two valleys. If a seam is going to leak, it leaks here, at 2,760 metres in sideways sleet, and we write down exactly where.

14 km+1,020 m

Day 3 · The knife-edge

Pack, meet rock

Four hours of scrambling on a ridge you can see both sides of. Hip belts get scraped, frames get loaded off-axis, and buckles learn what a dropped pack feels like.

12 km+680 m

Day 4 · Down to the lake

The knee report

1,800 metres of descent in one afternoon. Anything that rubs, bounces or slips gets marked with tape and goes straight back to the bench on Monday.

18 km−1,880 m

Repairs

Bring it back
broken

Anything with our ridge on it can come home to the workshop for life. Most repairs are free. The rest cost what the parts cost, and we'll tell you before we touch it.

  1. Post it or drop it offPrepaid label from our site, or knock on the workshop door in Larch Valley.
  2. We look within five daysEvery repair is logged on the product's serial, so we learn what fails.
  3. It comes back with a patchReinforced, re-taped, sometimes re-sewn — and stitched with the date it was fixed.
